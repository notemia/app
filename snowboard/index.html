How each lesson works

Most beginners hear what to do. This shows why it works.

“Weight forward” is hard to picture when you are sliding. Each lesson links four things, so a cue means something the next time you are on snow.

  1. Body

    What you do

    Move weight, flatten a foot, bend your knees.

  2. Board

    How it responds

    The edge grips or lets go. The nose drops. The board twists.

  3. Result

    What you see

    The track, the speed and the direction change.

  4. Feeling

    What you notice

    Note it in your own words and take one cue to your next day on snow.

Watch

Every turn in slow motion, step by step

Pause on any step, slow it to half speed, and switch between views from behind, in front and above. This is one half of a linked turn, in the order the app shows it:

  1. Traverse on your heels
  2. Look where you’re going next
  3. Weight toward the leading foot
  4. Leading foot flat, nose drops
  5. Flat board, nose in the fall line
  6. Hips smoothly over the toes
  7. Leading knee and hip steer the arc
  8. Across the slope, speed drops

Mistakes

See why the edge catches, before it catches you

The turn lessons play common mistakes next to the correct move, so you can see where they go wrong.

  • Changing edge earlyThe new edge goes down while the board still points across the slope. The snow stops the edge and the body keeps going.
  • Weight backThe tail lets go as the nose drops. Stay over the leading foot.

Rehearse on the ground

Short timed drills, with no board strapped on. A double vibration marks the next step, so you don’t have to watch the screen.

Afterwards, write down what you noticed. Your cues stay in My tips, ready for your next day on snow.

What this app is not

  • The scenes are simplified teaching models, not measurements.
  • No camera analysis and no pose detection. It cannot see you ride.
  • Finishing a lesson doesn’t mean you can do the move safely on snow.
  • It does not replace an instructor. Learn new moves with one, on a suitable beginner slope.
  • Use the app only while stopped.

Questions

Learning to snowboard with an app

Can you learn to snowboard from an app?

You can learn what each move is and what it does before you are on snow: how weight moves, why the nose drops, when the edge changes. Feeling it takes snow. Learn new moves with an instructor on a suitable beginner slope, and use the app to prepare and to reinforce what you learned.

How do I start if I have never been on a board?

Start with level one: the rules on the slope and how to fall and get up. Then the board and your stance, pushing with one foot and your first glides. All lessons stay open, so you can jump to what your next lesson covers.

How do you turn on a snowboard?

The app teaches this order: weight toward the leading foot, leading foot flat so the nose drops to the fall line, hips to the new edge, then flex and steer with the leading knee and hip. You can watch each step in slow motion and then steer it yourself in the model.

Why does my edge catch?

In one common case, the new edge goes down while the board still points across the slope. The snow stops the edge and the body keeps going. So: nose into the fall line first, edge change after. The app shows this mistake next to the correct move.

How should I fall on a snowboard?

Forward, onto your knees and forearms. Backward, onto your seat and back. The falling lesson shows both in 3D, along with what your arms mustn’t do and how to get up. No technique can guarantee a safe fall, and a helmet is strongly recommended by instructor schools.

Regular or goofy: how do I know my stance?

The app asks which foot leads. If you don’t know yet, it shows an example stance, clearly labeled, and you can change it later. Your writing hand doesn’t decide your stance.

Does the app use the camera or rate my riding?

No. It doesn’t use the camera, has no sensors on your board and can’t see how you ride. The score in the steering exercises is about the model only. It isn’t a skill rating.

Does it need internet or an account?

No. It works offline, with no account, ads, analytics or tracking. Your progress and notes stay on your iPhone.

Which devices and languages does it support?

iPhone with iOS 18 or later. English, Russian, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Simplified Chinese, Japanese and Korean.
