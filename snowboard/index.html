The game

Feel the board through your iPhone

It was hard to build, but we did it: tilt your iPhone and the board goes on its edge, carves an arc and takes off from a kicker. Five courses: groomers, a glacier, a park with a pipe, powder and a couloir.

  • Tilt left or right

    The board goes on its edge and turns. The more you tilt, the tighter the turn.

  • Smooth or sudden

    A smooth tilt carves and keeps your speed. A sudden one skids and slows you down.

  • Deep carve

    At speed the board lies right over. At low speed it can’t, as on a real slope.

  • Short squat

    A quick touch of the screen lightens the board: fast edge changes, short turns.

  • Long squat

    Hold a finger on the screen, then let go: that’s a jump.

  • In the air

    Tilt to spin and flip, hold a finger to grab. A clean landing scores.

Simplified physics, invented courses. One demo run is free; riding yourself comes with the purchase.

What the app doesn’t do

  • The scenes are simplified models. The game’s courses are invented.
  • It doesn’t use the camera and can’t see you ride.
  • Finishing a lesson or a course doesn’t mean you can do the move safely on snow. Don’t copy the game’s jumps, flips or speeds without training.
  • It doesn’t replace an instructor. Learn new moves with one, on a suitable slope.
  • Use the app only while stopped.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is there a game in the app too?

Yes: five courses, steered by tilting the phone. One demo run is free, and riding yourself comes with the purchase. It’s a game with simplified physics, not training.

How does steering by tilting the phone work?

Hold the phone however is comfortable: its position during the countdown becomes neutral. Tilt left or right to put the board on its edge and turn. Tip the top away for more speed. A finger on the screen squats, and letting go jumps. If tilt isn't for you, you can steer with a finger instead.

Can you learn to snowboard from an app?

You can learn what each move is and what it does: how weight moves, why the nose drops, when the edge changes. Feeling it takes snow, so learn new moves with an instructor and use the app to prepare.

How do I start if I have never been on a board?

Start with level one: the rules on the slope and how to fall and get up. Then the board and your stance, pushing with one foot and your first glides. These lessons are free.

How do you turn on a snowboard?

The app teaches this order: weight toward the leading foot, leading foot flat so the nose drops to the fall line, hips to the new edge, then flex and steer with the leading knee and hip. You can watch each step in slow motion and then steer it yourself in the model.

Why does my edge catch?

In one common case, the new edge goes down while the board still points across the slope. The snow stops the edge and the body keeps going. So: nose into the fall line first, edge change after. The app shows this mistake next to the correct move.

How should I fall on a snowboard?

Forward, onto your knees and forearms. Backward, onto your seat and back. The falling lesson shows both in 3D, along with what your arms mustn’t do and how to get up. No technique can guarantee a safe fall, and a helmet is strongly recommended by instructor schools.

Regular or goofy: how do I know my stance?

The app asks which foot leads. If you don’t know yet, it shows an example stance, clearly labeled, and you can change it later. Your writing hand doesn’t decide your stance.

Does the app use the camera or rate my riding?

No. It doesn’t use the camera, has no sensors on your board and can’t see how you ride. The score in the steering exercises is about the model only. It isn’t a skill rating.

Does it need internet or an account?

No. It works offline, with no account, ads, analytics or tracking. Your progress, notes and best scores stay on your iPhone.

Which devices and languages does it support?

iPhone with iOS 18 or later. English, Russian, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Simplified Chinese, Japanese and Korean.
